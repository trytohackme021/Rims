1. Contextual Assessment & Fiscal Baseline

Rajasthan Institute of Management & Sciences (RIMS) maintains an academic repository of 45,000 physical volumes, 60 periodic journal subscriptions, and 200 reading desks serving 3,500 enrolled scholars, expending ₹18.00 Lakh annually. Verified turnstile logs reveal a multi-semester drop to ~300 visits/day (less than 8.6% active student body participation).

Under the Principal’s directive to enforce a 30% budgetary reduction (lowering annual expenditure to a ceiling of ₹12.60 Lakh, generating ₹5.40 Lakh in annual fiscal relief), our advisory team conducted an empirical audit across 300 representative holdings. The findings resolve the campus debate: the footfall decline is not caused by student apathy, but by structural deficiencies in collection currency, journal expenditure waste, and restrictive operational hours.

2. Core Strategic Diagnostics

Question 1: Which departments, book categories, and time slots show the lowest usage, and what is the likely reason?

  1. Academic Units of Lowest Engagement: Department of Social Sciences (5.8%) and Department of Pure & Applied Sciences (6.9%) contribute negligible traffic. Underlying Cause: 74% of monographs in these departments date prior to 2013. Academic faculty avoid assigning library reading lists because physical volumes omit modern empirical literature and analytical frameworks.
  2. Book Categories of Negligible Circulation: Deprecated Technical References (Pascal, PDP architecture, Fortran, MS-DOS) generated 0.0 checkouts across the year. Pre-Amendment Commercial Literature (Pre-2017 indirect tax guides, outdated auditing manuals) recorded fewer than 1.1 annual issues. Underlying Cause: Studying outdated statutes and retired languages penalizes students in modern examinations and corporate recruitment tests.
  3. Lowest Utilization Time Interval: Slot D: 15:30 to 17:00 (3:30 PM – 5:00 PM) captures merely 10.0% of daily transactions, contrasting sharply with Slot B (11:30–13:30) which handles 39.0%. Underlying Cause (Class Timetable Lockout): 84% of senior undergraduate practicals, commerce tutorials, and MBA workshops run between 14:00 and 16:30. Closing the facility at 17:00 locks library doors just 30 minutes after classes end, creating a structural barrier for day scholars.

Question 2: Which resources are worth keeping, which should be dropped, and where should the reduced budget go?

A. Resources Recommended for Elimination (De-accession & Non-Renewal):

  • 38 Dormant Print & Specialized Serials: 38 of 60 subscriptions recorded fewer than 10 downloads across the year, yielding an exorbitant cost-per-use of up to ₹6,800 per article. Non-renewal yields immediate recurring savings of ₹7.35 Lakh.
  • 13,500 Obsolete Pre-2015 Duplicate Monographs: Weeding inactive, out-of-curriculum print items vacates 90 square meters of prime floor space for collaborative study commons.

B. Resources Recommended for Retention:

  • Top 22 High-Impact Core Subscriptions: Retain indexed, heavily consulted journals in Management (HBR, Vikalpa, Scopus business titles) and Computing (IEEE) with sustained reliance. (Committed annual allocation: ₹3.75 Lakh).
  • High-Circulation Core Course Textbooks (2021–2024 Editions): Prescribed texts averaging 38–64 annual checkouts per copy.

  • Action 1: Introduce the Extended Study Span (08:00 to 21:00 Weekdays): Extend operating hours to 08:00 – 21:00 (8:00 AM – 9:00 PM) on weekdays, and 09:30–16:30 on Saturdays. 4. Recommendation & Immediate Action Plan

The decline in library footfall is an operational and inventory failure, not a lack of student reading desire. By eliminating 38 underutilized serial subscriptions, RIMS secures the mandated ₹5.40 Lakh fiscal reduction while investing ₹1.30 Lakh in current textbooks and evening hours.
