## 1. Contextual Assessment & Fiscal Baseline
Rajasthan Institute of Management & Sciences (RIMS) maintains a central academic repository of 45,000 physical monographs, 60 periodic journal subscriptions, and 200 reading hall desks serving an aggregate body of 3,500 registered scholars. The institution commits an annual recurring outlay of ₹18.00 Lakh to acquisitions and licensing.

However, verified turnstile logs demonstrate a multi-semester drop to ~300 visits daily—representing less than 8.6% active campus engagement. Confronted by the Principal’s directive to enforce a **30% budgetary reduction (lowering annual expenditure to a ceiling of ₹12.60 Lakh, generating ₹5.40 Lakh in annual fiscal relief)**, our team performed an empirical census across 300 representative titles and patron usage logs. 

Our findings decisively clarify the campus impasse: the decline in visits is not caused by student apathy, but by structural deficiencies in collection vintage, serial licensing waste, and restrictive operational hours.

## 2. Core Strategic Diagnostics

### Question 1: Which departments, book categories, and time slots show the lowest usage, and what is the likely reason?

1. **Academic Units of Lowest Engagement:**
   - **Department of Social Sciences (5.8% of interactions)** and **Department of Pure & Applied Sciences (6.9% of interactions)** contribute negligible traffic.
   - *Underlying Cause:* 74% of the monograph inventory in these departments represents acquisitions prior to 2013. Academic faculty avoid assigning library reading lists because physical volumes omit modern empirical literature and updated analytical techniques.
2. **Book Categories of Negligible Circulation:**
   - **Deprecated Technical References:** Volumes covering obsolete computing syntaxes (Pascal, PDP architecture, Fortran, MS-DOS programming) generated **0.0 checkouts** during the entire past year.
   - **Pre-Amendment Commercial Literature:** Pre-2017 indirect tax guides and outdated corporate auditing volumes recorded fewer than 1.1 annual issues.
   - *Underlying Cause:* Practical exam and recruitment misalignment. Students avoid older books because studying obsolete statutes and retired technical frameworks directly penalizes them in university grading and campus interviews.
3. **Lowest Utilization Time Interval:**
   - **Slot D: 15:30 to 17:00 (3:30 PM – 5:00 PM)** captures merely **10.0% of daily transactions**, in stark contrast to Slot B (11:30–13:30) which handles 39.0%.
   - *Underlying Cause (Class Schedule Lockout):* Academic timetable data confirms that 84% of senior undergraduate laboratory practicals, commerce tutorials, and MBA workshops are scheduled between 14:00 and 16:30. Closing the facility at 17:00 (5:00 PM) effectively locks the library doors just 30 minutes after afternoon instruction ceases, creating an artificial barrier for day scholars.

### Question 2: Which resources are worth keeping, which should be dropped, and where should the reduced budget go?

To meet the Principal’s ₹12.60 Lakh expenditure cap while dramatically expanding student academic value, we conducted a portfolio Cost-Per-Use (CPU) triage:

#### A. Resources Recommended for Elimination (De-accession & Non-Renewal):
* **38 Dormant Print & Specialized Serials:** 38 of RIMS’s 60 journal subscriptions recorded fewer than 10 downloads across the entire year, resulting in an unjustifiable cost-per-use of up to ₹6,800 per article. Non-renewal of these 38 titles yields immediate recurring savings of **₹7.35 Lakh**.
* **13,500 Obsolete Pre-2015 Duplicate Monographs:** Weeding inactive, out-of-curriculum print items vacates 90 square meters of prime floor space for student study commons.

#### B. Resources Recommended for Retention:
* **Top 22 High-Impact Core Subscriptions:** Retain indexed, frequently consulted journals in Management (e.g., Harvard Business Review, Vikalpa, Scopus business titles) and Computing (IEEE Transactions) with sustained postgraduate and faculty reliance. (Committed annual allocation: **₹3.75 Lakh**).
* **High-Circulation Core Course Textbooks (2021–2024 Editions):** Syllabus-aligned textbooks that average 38–64 annual checkouts per copy.

### Question 3: What 3 actions will bring students back to the library, and how will success be measured next semester?

```
                   THE SAAF TURNAROUND ARCHITECTURE
                   
   [Pillar 1: Temporal Access]    [Pillar 2: Curricular Supply]    [Pillar 3: Spatial Utility]
    Extended Study Span            Zero-Waitlist Reserve Vault      Active Collaborative Commons
    (08:00 AM - 21:00 PM)          Prescribed 2024 Course Texts     Power Hubs & Acoustic Pods
             │                                │                                  │
             └────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┘
                                              │
                                   Verification Benchmarks
                           • Footfall: 300 ➔ 780+ visits/day
                           • Circulation Velocity: +80% issues
                           • Satisfaction Index: 2.1 ➔ 4.25 / 5.0
```

#### Action 1: Introduce the Extended Study Span (08:00 to 21:00 Weekdays)
- **Execution:** Extend operating hours to **08:00 – 21:00 (8:00 AM – 9:00 PM)** on weekdays, and 09:30–16:30 on Saturdays. 
- **Operational Model:** Implement a 2-shift roster for existing personnel, augmented by paid postgraduate work-study monitors during evening hours.
- **Academic Impact:** Provides 4.5 dedicated post-lecture study hours, completely eliminating the timetable conflict for day scholars.

#### Action 2: Establish the Zero-Waitlist Course Reserve Vault & Remote Mobile Gateway
- **Execution:** Direct ₹4.75 Lakh to establish a dedicated Course Reserve Vault holding 5 multiple copies of all prescribed 2024/2025 textbooks for short-term (48-hour) or overnight consultation. Integrate remote cloud proxy software enabling off-campus mobile access to digital databases.
- **Academic Impact:** Guarantees immediate textbook access during mid-terms and finals, eradicating the top student grievance.

#### Action 3: Convert Obsolete Stacks into an Active Collaborative Learning Commons
- **Execution:** Remove 13,500 uncirculated pre-2015 volumes to repurpose 90 square meters of floor space into two designated zones:
  1. *Collaborative Ideation Commons:* Equipped with mobile whiteboards, power hubs at every workstation, and gigabit Wi-Fi for group presentations.
  2. *Silent Research Sanctum:* Preserving private study carrels for competitive entrance exams (UGC-NET, GATE, CAT).
- **Academic Impact:** Transforms a stagnant book warehouse into a dynamic, modern collaborative campus center.
