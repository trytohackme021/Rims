## 1. Figure 1: Academic School Utilization Breakdown
**Visual Link:** [figure_1_department_utilization.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/rims_library_consulting_taskforce/analysis/figure_1_department_utilization.png)  
**Focal Metric:** Total Physical Checkouts vs. IP-Authenticated Electronic Downloads across 5 academic schools.

### Executive Insight (1–2 Lines):
> **Finding:** *The School of Business and School of Technology & Computing generate 71.4% of total institutional interactions, propelled by intense computing e-resource traffic. In contrast, Social Sciences and Pure & Applied Sciences contribute under 13% combined due to obsolete monograph inventories and zero licensed e-packages.*

*Core Audit Metrics:*
- School of Business: 1,120 physical checkouts, 540 electronic downloads.
- School of Technology & Computing: 890 physical checkouts, 1,360 electronic downloads.
- Social Sciences & Applied Sciences: Combined annual checkouts average under 4.1 per title.

## 2. Figure 2: Monograph Circulation Velocity vs. Edition Vintage
**Visual Link:** [figure_2_issues_vs_publication_year.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/rims_library_consulting_taskforce/analysis/figure_2_issues_vs_publication_year.png)  
**Focal Metric:** Annual circulation frequency plotted against imprint edition year.

### Executive Insight (1–2 Lines):
> **Finding:** *A severe "vintage threshold" is evident: monographs published prior to 2015 average merely 1.34 checkouts annually, confirming student assertions that the collection is obsolete. Conversely, 2021–2024 editions circulate at peak velocity (up to 64 issues/copy), proving that students actively utilize current syllabus materials.*

*Core Audit Metrics:*
- Pre-2015 Shelf Share: 42% of physical monographs surveyed.
- Zero-Circulation Titles: 58 legacy items recorded 0 checkouts in the past 12 months.
- 2022–2024 Editions: Exceed 40 checkouts/year on average, operating with waiting queues.

## 3. Figure 3: Portfolio Efficiency Matrix (Annual Cost vs. Utilization Yield)
**Visual Link:** [figure_3_cost_vs_usage.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/rims_library_consulting_taskforce/analysis/figure_3_cost_vs_usage.png)  
**Focal Metric:** Total annual holding cost plotted against aggregate usage yield (Issues + Downloads).

### Executive Insight (1–2 Lines):
> **Finding:** *Print and specialized serial subscriptions represent the primary financial leakage, absorbing over 64% of collection budgets while 57% record under 10 downloads annually (costing up to ₹6,800/retrieval). Core course textbooks and bundled digital e-resources generate over 14x greater cost-efficiency.*

*Core Audit Metrics:*
- High-Cost / Low-Yield Serials: 53 of 93 sampled subscriptions registered <10 downloads.
- Average Journal Subscription Outlay: ₹54,800 per title.
- High-Efficiency Reserve Books: Average cost-per-consultation is ₹22 – ₹65.

## 4. Figure 4: Operating Hours Inundation & Late Afternoon Chokepoint
**Visual Link:** [figure_4_visits_by_time_slot.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/rims_library_consulting_taskforce/analysis/figure_4_visits_by_time_slot.png)  
**Focal Metric:** Footfall distribution across operating intervals between 09:00 and 17:00.

### Executive Insight (1–2 Lines):
> **Finding:** *Library footfall peaks heavily during Midday Slot B (11:30–13:30 at 39%), followed by a steep 74% collapse in late afternoon Slot D (10%) due to mandatory laboratory practicals running until 16:30. Terminating operations at 17:00 locks out 2,200 day scholars just as their academic classes end.*

*Core Audit Metrics:*
- Slot A (09:00 - 11:30): 99 recorded interactions (33.0%).
- Slot B (11:30 - 13:30): 117 recorded interactions (39.0%) [Peak Period].
- Slot C (13:30 - 15:30): 54 recorded interactions (18.0%).
- Slot D (15:30 - 17:00): 30 recorded interactions (10.0%) [Post-lab Choke].

## 5. Figure 5: Student Satisfaction Index Across Seniority Cohorts
**Visual Link:** [figure_5_satisfaction_by_year.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/rims_library_consulting_taskforce/analysis/figure_5_satisfaction_by_year.png)  
**Focal Metric:** Exit survey Likert scores (1 to 5) segmented from Freshman UG to Master Level PG-2.

### Executive Insight (1–2 Lines):
> **Finding:** *User sentiment drops steadily from Freshman UG (mean: 3.71/5.0) down to Master Level PG-2 (mean: 2.08/5.0). While junior undergraduates find foundational study desks adequate, senior and master-level scholars preparing dissertations face severe deficits in research-grade monographs and remote database access.*

*Core Audit Metrics:*
- Year 1 (Freshman UG): Mean score 3.71 / 5.0.
- Year 2 & Year 3 UG: Mean score 2.84 / 5.0.
- Master Level (PG-1 & PG-2): Mean score 2.08 – 2.19 / 5.0 (Severe Research Dissatisfaction).
